What it demonstrates:

### Who’s using it

The primary user is a Product Manager reviewing customer feedback and deciding where product attention is needed. Juno would be most useful at the start of the week, before roadmap or prioritization reviews, and whenever a PM notices feedback starting to accumulate around a topic but does not yet know whether it represents a meaningful signal.

Instead of manually checking support tickets, Slack threads, account notes, and Jira issues, the PM opens Juno to see which customer problems are growing, how much existing revenue is associated with them, which opportunities are affected, and what has materially changed since the previous review.

### Where you’d see it

Juno appears as a **Customer Signal Dashboard** that the PM can open and explore.

The first view is a ranked table of signal buckets, ordered by existing customer revenue. Each row shows the customer problem, whether it is an issue or enhancement, whether the signal is new or existing, number of affected customers, existing revenue, associated opportunities, opportunity value, recent changes, and Juno’s suggested urgency.

Changes are visible directly in the table, for example **6 customers ▲1** or **€2.0M ▲€420K**, so the PM can immediately see where a signal is gaining importance.

The PM can expand any row to review the customers and opportunities behind the signal, why Juno suggested its urgency, and the supporting evidence from exact Jira tickets, Slack channels, and Notion pages.

### What it takes off your plate

Today, a PM has to manually collect and reconcile fragmented feedback across multiple sources, work out which comments describe the same underlying problem, remove duplicates, identify which customers and opportunities are involved, look up their commercial value, and compare the result with previous weeks.

Juno automates that synthesis and gives the PM a continuously structured view of customer signals. The PM no longer spends hours building the picture; instead, they spend their time interpreting the signals and deciding what deserves product action.

### Framework bucket

**Primary: 🔍 Insights**

The core value is making patterns visible that would otherwise remain buried across hundreds of fragmented customer signals. Juno turns individual anecdotes into ranked, evidence-backed product themes and highlights which ones are becoming more commercially significant.

**Secondary: 💸 Automation**

Juno automates much of the manual work required to collect, deduplicate, group, quantify, and update customer feedback across Jira, Slack, and Notion.

**Secondary: 🚀 Augmentation**

The PM still makes the product judgment and prioritization decision, but Juno dramatically shortens the analysis required to get there.

**Not core to the MVP: ✨ Personalization**

The dashboard could later be personalized by product area, customer segment, geography, or PM ownership, but personalization is not necessary for the initial value proposition.
